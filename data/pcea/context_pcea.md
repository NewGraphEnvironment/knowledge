# PCEA (Peace Arm) — field-prep area context

Watershed group **PCEA / Peace Arm** = the Peace-River arm of **Williston Reservoir**, near
**Mackenzie, BC** (Omineca / Region 7A). Formed by the W.A.C. Bennett Dam (1968). This brief is the
"learn everything first" layer: who's here, what's been done, who cares, and what to read before the field.

> Status: draft from web + EcoCat + PSCIS pull, 2026-07-10. Citations inline. Reports flagged
> **[grabbable]** have a working direct-PDF path (proven downloadable).

## Nations & territory (engagement-critical)

Williston Reservoir sits on the traditional territories of the **Tse Keh Nay / Sekani** peoples —
**Tsay Keh Dene Nation**, **Kwadacha First Nation**, and **McLeod Lake Indian Band** — within
**Treaty 8**. ([BC Treaty Commission — Tsay Keh Dene](https://bctreaty.ca/tsay-keh-dene-nation/),
[McLeod Lake Indian Band](https://www.mlib.ca/about/), [Wikipedia — Tsay Keh Dene](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Tsay_Keh_Dene_First_Nation))

- The reservoir flooded the Rocky Mountain Trench, inundating Sekani settlements and traplines near
  **Fort Grahame and Finlay Forks**; ~40–50 Tsay Keh Dene (then "Ingenika") members were relocated.
  This is living history — approach reservoir-tributary work with that grievance in mind.
- McLeod Lake Tse'khene joined Treaty 8 by adhesion (advised Canada in 1987; adhesion 2000).

## Past initiatives, research & funding

- **Fish & Wildlife Compensation Program – Peace Region** (formerly the **Peace/Williston Fish &
  Wildlife Compensation Program, PWFWCP**) is the dominant funder/actor — compensates BC Hydro dam
  impacts, tributary-focused. 24 Peace-region projects funded 2024–25.
  ([FWCP regions](https://fwcp.ca/our-work-in-your-region/), [FWCP funding priorities](https://fwcp.ca/funding/fundingpriorities.html))

## Local groups & interests

- **Mackenzie Fish & Game Association** — the primary local stewardship group (fish/game recreation,
  marksmanship, wildlife & habitat conservation). Natural first contact.
  ([Facebook](https://www.facebook.com/MFGA.Mack/))
- Regional: **North Peace Rod & Gun Club** (Fort St. John), [BC Wildlife Federation clubs](https://bcwf.bc.ca/our-clubs/).
- Williston is a heavily used recreational fishery — **bull trout, rainbow trout, lake & rocky
  mountain whitefish** most prevalent. District of Mackenzie publishes a [fishing guide](https://districtofmackenzie.ca/wp-content/uploads/2018/06/FishingGuide.pdf).
  Angling regs: [Region 7A Omineca synopsis](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/sports-recreation-arts-and-culture/outdoor-recreation/fishing-and-hunting/freshwater-fishing/region_7a_omineca.pdf).

## IPCAs, protected areas, listed species & connectivity

**Indigenous Protected & Conserved Areas (Tsay Keh Dene-led):**
- **Ingenika IPCA** — declared by Tsay Keh Dene in **2020**, in the Ingenika watershed (north end of
  Williston). Valued for rare/diverse topography, carbon storage, and climate-resilient habitat for
  **wolverine, grizzly bear, caribou**; boundaries refined with **connectivity analysis**.
  ([Y2Y](https://y2y.net/blog/actionable-strategies-to-conserve-changing-landscapes/),
  [IPCA Knowledge Basket](https://ipcaknowledgebasket.ca/systematic-conservation-planning-in-tsay-keh-dene-territory/))
- **Chuyaza Conservancy** — second Tsay Keh Dene protected area (2022 High Conservation Value
  assessment; management plans in development for provincial recognition).
  ([Earthworm](https://earthworm.org/news-stories/tsay-keh-dene-nation-leads-pulp-companies-in-a-landscape-initiative))

**Provincial parks / protected areas around Williston:**
- **Omineca Provincial Park & Protected Area** (est. 2001) — riparian/wetland habitat on the Omineca
  River (Omineca Arm). ([Wikipedia](https://en.wikipedia.org/wiki/Omineca_Provincial_Park_and_Protected_Area))
- Parks along the reservoir: **Muscovite Lakes, Butler Ridge, Heather–Dina Lakes, Ed Bird–Estella**.
  The **Muskwa-Kechika Management Area** lies further north ([M-K backcountry plan DRAFT](https://engage.gov.bc.ca/app/uploads/sites/121/2026/02/BCParks_M-K_Backcountry_Plan_DRAFT.pdf)).

**Listed / at-risk species (field + permit relevance):**
- **Bull Trout — Western Arctic population (DU2)** — COSEWIC **Special Concern** (2025); Mackenzie/Peace
  system. Threats: fragmented habitat, non-native species.
  ([COSEWIC 2025](https://www.canada.ca/en/environment-climate-change/services/species-risk-public-registry/cosewic-assessments-status-reports/bull-trout-5-populations-2025.html))
- **Arctic Grayling (Williston watershed)** — at risk; vulnerable to angling pressure + habitat loss.
- **Woodland Caribou** — **Chase herd** (west side of Williston; Ingenika/Mesilinka/Osilinka/Omineca
  watersheds) and **Upper Finlay herd** ([boundary refinement report](https://www2.gov.bc.ca/assets/gov/environment/plants-animals-and-ecosystems/wildlife-wildlife-habitat/caribou/wii_report457_herd_boundary_refinement_upper_finaly_caribou_herd_2013.pdf)); plus grizzly, wolverine.

**Connectivity corridors:**
- Tsay Keh Dene used connectivity analysis to identify **grizzly movement corridors** (camera-trap
  verification) and restoration priorities near **Johanson Lake, Tenakihi FSR, Tutizzi Lake, Swannell
  River** — within the Chase caribou range.
- **Aquatic connectivity note:** larger tributaries — **Halfway and Pine** — hold abundant coldwater
  habitat; resident bull trout / Arctic grayling there recruit to Peace River populations by
  downstream dispersal. (Directly reinforces prioritizing barrier removal on those systems — and why
  **PINE** is the richer target.)
- FWCP connectivity report **[grabbable]** — `a100.gov.bc.ca/pub/acat/documents/r57729/PEA_F19_W_2650_...pdf`
  ([direct PDF](https://a100.gov.bc.ca/pub/acat/documents/r57729/PEA_F19_W_2650_1574271681568_4265887703.pdf))

## What the PSCIS data says (thin)

22 PSCIS assessments, **all "Trib to Clearwater Creek"** (stream_crossing_id 7818–784x), 20 BARRIER /
1 PASSABLE / 1 POTENTIAL, from a **single 2010 assessment** (ref PSCIS77). No habitat confirmations,
designs, or remediations on record. So formal fish-passage work in PCEA is light — the priority
picture will come mostly from the **grayling/tributary** literature above + our own model, not PSCIS.

## Cautions / open questions

- **"Clearwater" name collision:** the well-documented **Marjorie Creek / Clearwater *Lake*** LWD
  restoration (BioTerra 1998) is in the **Cariboo** region — almost certainly a *different* Clearwater
  than PCEA's "Trib to Clearwater Creek" near Mackenzie. Do **not** cite it as PCEA context until the
  PCEA crossing coordinates (in `crossings.csv`) are checked against that watershed.
- **PSCIS `ecocat_url` is unreliable** — the PCEA link (`[PSCIS77]`) returns 0 EcoCat results.
  Keyword/GNIS-name search (EcoCat + Google) is the working path, not the stored link.
- **CLIR** search app looks session-based — may need the browser tool; EcoCat + Google cover most of
  what it aggregates.
