# What the past reports say — Lower North Thompson (LNTH)

Read 2026-09-08 from the EcoCat reports that PSCIS links to or that an EcoCat keyword search
places in the North Thompson (issue #11). Per-report notes are in
`data/thompson/report_notes/<id>.md`; the link table is at the end. Cite reports by EcoCat id.

## First, what is *not* in the reports

PSCIS records for LNTH point at 21 distinct `[PSCISnn]` project tags. **Only three have an EcoCat
report behind them** (24097 Trumbley 2008, 24146 Triton 2010, 24103 Adams Lake 2008). The
campaigns behind most of the assessed crossings — Fisheries and Oceans 2021–22 (95 LNTH
crossings, [PSCIS4740]), Braumandl 2018–2020 (82 crossings including every LNTH habitat
confirmation, [PSCIS3400/3740/3940]), the untagged 2010 batch (43, [PSCIS77]), Canadian Wildlife
Federation 2019–2023 (assessment, confirmation, design and remediation, [PSCIS4500/4521/4522]),
Cornice 2025 and the 2007 Integrated ProAction remediations — have **no narrative report in
EcoCat**. For those, PSCIS itself is the record; there is nothing to read.

So the readable corpus is older (2003–2011) and FIA-era, plus a few 2020s permit returns. It
tells you where the barrier problems were found and what was fixed before PSCIS existed, not what
the 2018–2025 assessors concluded.

## What people said, by sub-basin

**Fennell Creek / Barriere River.** The 2003 coho barrier screen (24379, Secwepemc Fisheries
Commission for Tolko) walked every Crown forest-road crossing on six coho-producing tributaries.
On Fennell Creek it found two collapsed 1960s log box culverts on a non-status road off the North
Barriere Lake FSR (Sites 5 and 6, "requires removal ASAP", excellent coho rearing and spawning
habitat above) and a graded-over 800 mm culvert (Site 4) that is moot because a 32 % gradient
sits 40 m downstream. **Sites 5 and 6 were removed in August 2004** (25657, $13,267, post-and-brush
revetments) — any barrier record there is stale. Open from that report: a possible log-jam
barrier near km 12 of Fennell Creek above more than 10 km of good habitat, never assessed.

**Mann Creek and the TFL 18 lakes country (Canfor Vavenby).** The 2008 FPCI on TFL 18 (24097,
Trumbley) scored 90 crossings under the 2008 MOE protocol; the Lower North Thompson sites are
tributaries to Mann Creek and the Moira, Hegar, Calvin, Cathy and Moose Lake Creek drainages off
Roads 2, 6, 7A, 10, 91 and 208. Twenty confirmed-fish barriers, and Canfor's four Priority 1
replacements are all Mann Creek tributaries: LNT-R6-2 (3.6 km habitat gained, bridge ~$85K),
LNT-R208-1, LNT-R6-12K (score 39, 41 cm drop) and the Calvin Lake outlet LNT-R6-1. Two large-gain
Moira Lake tributaries (LNT-R91-1, LNT-R91-2, 3.5–4.1 km) were second tier or needed fish
presence confirmed. Just as useful is its non-priority table: many scored barriers have a natural
barrier within a few hundred metres, which is why they were dropped — a model re-flagging them
should lose that argument. The 2011 stream classification (24281) adds that the Road 122 culvert
on the Cindy Lake outlet (Mann system) is perched, and that a fishless direct North Thompson
tributary off the Caribou Mountain FSR (S258) implies an unlocated barrier downstream in the
Weyerhaeuser area. These 2008 sites were assessed under the protocol PSCIS adopted, so most are
probably in PSCIS by coordinate; check which of the Priority 1 sites were replaced after 2008.

**Louis Creek (Tolko).** The 2009/10 assessment (24146, Triton) is a negative result: 101
modelled crossings screened to 16 possibly fish-bearing, only 6 with structures, the Crown
crossings of Louis Creek itself are bridges, and the single barrier (site 53, 145 cm drop,
ephemeral tributary) is on a private woodlot road and FIA-ineligible. Its appendix classifies all
101 modelled sites, which is a ready answer for model-flagged crossings on Louis and Fraser
creeks. What it does not cover is the valley-bottom private-land and highway crossings, which is
where any remaining Louis Creek passage question lives.

**Lemieux Creek and the lower coho tributaries.** 24379 found no Crown forest-road culvert
problems on Lemieux Creek (bridges, mostly on private or highway tenure) or Reg Christie Creek
(falls 600 m above the confluence limit coho). Lion Creek was never reached.

**Mainstem context.** The CN Rail Mile 59.0 habitat-compensation series near Birch Island
(16391, 16531, 16850, 17242; 2004–2008) has no crossings in it, but it is the best local
statement of what uses North Thompson side channels: coho catch rose from 28/74 to 91/175
(July/October) over five years, Chinook, bull trout and rainbow present, coho spawning seen in
2005 and 2007. Tributary crossings entering this reach are inside Interior Fraser coho habitat.

## Reports fetched that turned out to be elsewhere or not usable

24103 (Adams Lake, South Thompson), 25672 (George Creek, Adams group per FWA), 53746 (a
Clearwater Creek in the Peace region), 23788 (Thompson Brook, Kelowna). 11346 is 24 handwritten
fish-collection forms whose text is illegible; query FISS by permit instead. Five permit returns
(58437, 58812, 42636, 64523, 64642) carry data attachments but no PDF, and 54400 (the 1974
inventory) and 369 (steelhead telemetry) are unscanned images. The 2020 Cornice habitat
compensation monitoring (59209) has no PDF attachment either.

## What a planner should take from this

1. Treat the DFO 2021–22, Braumandl 2018–20, CWF 2019–23 and Cornice 2025 PSCIS records as the
   current word on their crossings; no report will contradict or explain them.
2. Before re-prioritising anything on TFL 18 Roads 6 and 208 (Mann Creek), find out which of
   Canfor's 2008 Priority 1 sites were replaced.
3. Fennell Creek's two box culverts are gone; the km 12 log jam is the open question there.
4. Louis Creek's Crown crossings are settled; the model's 101 sites are mostly phantoms.
5. Everything here predates the habitat confirmations; the three confirmation records in PSCIS
   (Braumandl 2018/2020, CWF 2019) are the only Phase 2 work and have no report.
