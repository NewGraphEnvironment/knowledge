# What the past reports say — Upper North Thompson (UNTH)

Read 2026-09-08 from the EcoCat reports that PSCIS links to or that an EcoCat keyword search
places in the North Thompson (issue #11). Per-report notes are in
`data/thompson/report_notes/<id>.md`; the link table is at the end. Cite reports by EcoCat id.

## First, what is *not* in the reports

PSCIS records for UNTH point at 8 `[PSCISnn]` project tags and **none of them has an EcoCat
report**: Fisheries and Oceans 2021–22 ([PSCIS4740], 57 crossings), Cornice 2025 ([PSCIS6300/6301],
15), Secwepemc Fisheries Commission 2003 ([PSCIS73]), Snowy River 2005–06 ([PSCIS86/88]), MoTI
2024 ([PSCIS5981]) and the 2007 Integrated ProAction remediations ([PSCIS254]). Everything
readable came from the keyword and consultant-name searches. For the modern assessments, PSCIS is
the record.

## What people said, by sub-basin

**Raft River tributaries, Hole in the Wall FSR (Canfor Vavenby).** The 2006 FPCI (24436, Snowy
River) is the one thorough Phase 1 in the group: 26 crossings on unnamed Raft River tributaries
northeast of Clearwater. Twenty were certified non fish-streams (gradients of 23–47 % or channel
dissipation 50–110 m below the road), with upper-limit-of-fish coordinates that can pre-empt
field visits. Six carried fish (rainbow trout only): Site 13 a full barrier (68 cm drop, FPCI 42,
fish 350 m above, 1.7–1.8 km gained), Site 15 partial (FPCI 38, natural falls 575 m up), Sites
16, 17 and 19 partial, and Blowhole Creek (Site 26) a velocity barrier at FPCI 31 that FIA
declined to fund. **Sites 13 and 15 were replaced with multi-plate arches in September–October
2007** (25682, Integrated ProAction; these are almost certainly the two [PSCIS254] remediation
records). Blowhole Creek was never fixed and Site 16 was recommended for an arch for capacity
reasons — those two are the live items on that road.

**Upper North Thompson above Clearwater and the Albreda (coho tributaries).** The 2003 coho
barrier screen (24379) identified 38 crossings on the North Thompson mainstem tributaries above
the Albreda confluence and 12 on the Albreda; most were bridges, dry, or not coho habitat. Three
North Thompson FSR culverts (Sites 12, 16, 20: 35–60 cm drops, scores 29–42) and one on the Allan
Creek FSR (Albreda Site 1, score 39, excellent rearing habitat above) were rated barriers, but the
authors say plainly the scores assume multiple species and that fish presence sampling is needed
before any of them is called a real barrier. Two known-coho sites were never assessed: Pleasant
Creek (Site 7A, elliptical culvert blocked by a debris flow diverting the creek down the road,
coho in the reach) and Dora Creek on the Albreda (ATV access only). Blue River had nothing needing
work. These are pre-PSCIS and located by lat/long and road km; matching them to PSCIS is by
position only.

**Blue River area.** Two wood box culverts on Smoke Creek FSR branches 5 and 7 (an unnamed
tributary system, WSC 129-700700, near Blue River) were removed during road deactivation in
September 2025 (64643, Hill Environmental). No fish were seen in the dewatered channels and the
sites did not match any PSCIS crossing within 300 m; treat them as open channel.

**Mainstem context.** Interior Fraser coho, Chinook, bull trout and rainbow use North Thompson
side channels (the CN Mile 59 series at Birch Island, 2004–2008, summarised in the LNTH file);
the 2003 screen's premise was that five tributaries — Albreda, Blue River, Fennell, Lemieux and
the upper mainstem — produced 38 % of North Thompson coho escapement in 2001.

## Reports fetched that turned out to be elsewhere or not usable

Same set as LNTH: 24103 and 25672 (Adams), 53746 (Peace), 23788 (Kelowna), 11346 (illegible
scan), the five permit returns with no PDF, and the two unscanned images (54400, 369).

## What a planner should take from this

1. The DFO 2021–22 and Cornice 2025 records are the only modern assessments and have no report;
   read them from PSCIS.
2. On the Hole in the Wall FSR, Sites 13 and 15 are done; Blowhole Creek (Site 26) and Site 16
   are the ones the 2006 assessment wanted fixed and nobody funded.
3. The 2003 North Thompson FSR and Allan Creek FSR culverts need fish sampling before they are
   barriers; Pleasant Creek (coho, debris flow) was flagged in 2003 and never assessed in any
   report here.
4. Most upper-valley tributary crossings on Crown roads were bridges or on non-habitat in 2003,
   which is an argument against model-flagged crossings there until PSCIS says otherwise.
