## What was done
Scope widened from earlier years to three sites (p. 2): Site C (new back channel, 2003), Site A (Cross back channel bank stabilization, 2003–04) and Site B (Cross back channel rock spurs, log structures and setback dyke built spring 2006 as compensation for the Mile 59.1 bridge; target 240 m² instream habitat and 1800 m² riparian). 28 minnow-trap stations (23 in Site C, 1 on the North Thompson mainstem, 1 at Site A, 3 at Site B), 3 baited traps per station for ~24 h, July 13–16 (72 trap-days in Site C, 15 in the Cross back channel) and October 18–23, 2007; water quality at each station; hard-structure inspection with video/photo documentation; soft-structure rating; ~5 h angling per session; redd search in October; revegetation survey (pp. 3–5). No road crossings named.

## Findings
- Site C fish (pp. 11–12): July — 30 coho (0.4/trap-day), 2 Chinook, total 42 fish; below 2006 (54 coho) and similar to 2004–05. October — 99 coho (1.4/trap-day), 16 Chinook, total 193 fish (2006: 114 coho, 210 total). Captured-plus-observed coho ~101 (July) and ~118 (October), similar to 2006. Root-wad/stump pool complexes accounted for ~2/3 of coho captures; the deep armoured section (structures 1–10) consistently poor.
- Cross back channel fish (p. 12): July 1 coho (0.1/trap-day), total 3 fish; October 11 coho (0.7/trap-day), total 153 fish (mostly redside shiners), mostly beside root wads above and below Site B; habitat judged more effective than before the works.
- Angling/observations (p. 16): several bull trout in the mainstem back-eddy at the intake groyne; in October one adult male coho (~13 kg) caught near Structure 4; **4 adult coho and 2 redds observed below Structure 15 near station 23** in Site C; rainbow trout of 50, 45 and 35 cm and bull trout of 65 and 50 cm caught below structure 1. The 2007 freshet cleared some Cross back channel gravels, which may again be usable by Chinook and coho.
- Structures (pp. 6–8, 17): 600 mm intake pipe still not evident; fish passage at the intake possible at moderate–high river levels but limited at low water; low-water inlet-culvert velocity 20 cm/s; sedimentation continuing to infill the deep overwinter channel. Site A riprap slid, exposing filter fabric; erosion and tree loss downstream of Site A. Site B spurs and log structures stable and collecting debris; Site B/dyke plantings largely failed in the dry 2006–07 seasons.
- Water quality within coho optima; cattle still entering the site.

## Recommendations and priorities
(pp. 19–20) Bulleted: clean and/or repair the rock sieve to restore fish access and flow to the culvert intake, or if the 600 mm pipe cannot be replaced, improve rock and pool structures downstream of the intake culvert to aid passage at high water; rebury exposed filter fabric at Site A with riprap; monitor bank erosion between Sites A and B; replant the Site B dyke. Continue monitoring; future structures to be large log-jam/root-wad complexes beside 1–3 m² pools 50 cm–1 m deep; bathymetric survey of the overwinter channel; place willow stakes near the water table. No costs given.

## Useful for crossing work today
Not a crossing inventory and not PSCIS data. Its use for passage planning is as fish-presence and habitat evidence: coho spawning and rearing, Chinook rearing, and large bull trout and rainbow trout in North Thompson side channels at Mile 59 in 2007, plus a 2006 data point (54/114 coho) otherwise unavailable in this set. It also records that the Site B works were compensation for the CN Mile 59.1 bridge, i.e. a rail crossing was rebuilt at Mile 59.1 around 2005–06, which may matter if a rail crossing near there appears in the crossing layer.
