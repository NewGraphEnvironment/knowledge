## What was done
Two unnamed direct tributaries to the Raft River (S3 streams supporting rainbow trout), Site 13 at km 13.4 and Site 15 at km 12.7 of the Hole in the Wall FSR (UTM zone 11: 314207E 5738456N and 314410E 5737923N), had their culverts removed and replaced with multi-plate SPCSP arch structures (3050 × 1350 mm × 13.0 m, drawing E8073; 3350 × 1360 mm × 14.0 m, drawing E8074) on precast footings, with streambed reconstruction (sand, gravel, large boulders placed to form step-pools), rip-rap at inlet and outlet, and approach fills raised. Contractor Wadlegger Logging & Construction, Clearwater. Fish salvage and dewatering preceded construction.

## Findings
From the 2005 Snowy River FPCI: Site 13 was a full barrier (68 cm outfall drop), Site 15 a partial barrier (22 cm drop); both culverts were undersized for Q100 and too short for the road prism; fish were confirmed upstream of both. FPCI scores 42 and 38, both Priority Class 1. Upstream habitat gained: 1,800 m (site 13) and 575 m (site 15, above which a permanent natural barrier exists). The abstract reports the works completed and habitat improved; no post-construction monitoring data is given (a cost summary section follows the text extracted).

## Recommendations and priorities
The 2005 assessment recommended replacing both with arch structures or low-profile bridges; this abstract documents that it was done. No further recommendations.

## Useful for crossing work today
These two Raft River tributary crossings were remediated in 2007 and should appear in PSCIS as remediations ([PSCIS250] Integrated ProAction 2007 links three remediation records in LNTH; these are likely two of them). Anything in PSCIS still showing them as barriers is stale. The report is the local worked example of the FPCI → site plan → arch replacement chain, with the 2006 Snowy River supplementary site-plan document (TFL 18 n=3, Raft River n=2) as the design reference.
