No FIA/FRBC/DFO funding stated.
- **Region check:** in the North Thompson — Upper North Thompson (UNTH). The two crossings are on an "unnamed tributary to an unnamed tributary (WSC 129-700700) to the North Thompson River (WSC 129)" on branches 5 and 7 of the Smoke Creek FSR, reached 95.2 km north of Clearwater on Highway 5 then 12.4 km up the Smoke Creek FSR (p. 4). Branch 5 crossing UTM 11U 346399 5774866 (≈52.10 N, 119.24 W, near Blue River); branch 7 crossing UTM 11U 346679 5774365 (p. 4). Note the introduction (p. 4) gives a different branch-7 UTM (11U 353166 5789163), ~16 km away; the section 1.1 coordinates are consistent with the described access route and each other.
- **Type:** remediation (environmental monitoring of box-culvert removal during road deactivation); no fish passage assessment.

## What was done
Hill Environmental acted as QEP to monitor removal of two wood box culverts as part of the ~1.5 km deactivation of branches 5 and 7 of the Smoke Creek FSR (p. 4). Branch 7 was removed September 15, 2025 and branch 5 on September 16, 2025, inside the July 22 – September 30 least-risk window (p. 8). Work sequence at each site: upstream sump and pump diversion with fish-friendly intake, channel dewatered and checked for fish, fill removed from behind sill logs, cross logs then sill logs extracted by excavator, excess material scraped from channel, channel washed, turbidity monitored upstream/midstream/downstream until near background (pp. 8–10, Table 1 pp. 11–12). At both sites the QEP judged flows too low to warrant electrofishing or a downstream sump (pp. 8, 9). At branch 5, three large logs placed at the culvert inlet "by a local skiing company" were removed because they were obstructing bedload transport (p. 9). Streams named: only the unnamed tributary (WSC 129-700700); Smoke Creek is named only as the FSR. Road names: Smoke Creek FSR branches 5 and 7. No PSCIS crossing ids given.

## Findings
- No fish were reported during monitoring of the dewatered channels (pp. 8, 9); no fish species are named anywhere in the report.
- Habitat: substrate cobble-dominated, gravel sub-dominant at both crossings (pp. 8, 9). At branch 7 "a steep cascade, likely caused by obstruction of bedload movement from the culvert, was observed at the upstream end of the culvert inlet" (p. 8) — i.e. the box culvert had been aggrading the channel above it.
- Turbidity: branch 7 midstream peaked at 70.8 NTU average during channel washing (baseline 0.14 NTU) and fell to 9.29 NTU by 16:54; branch 5 downstream peaked at 18.91 NTU (baseline 0.16) and fell to 7.56 NTU by 16:02 (Table 1, pp. 11–12). Two sump breaches on branch 5 (pump out of fuel / mechanical failure) flushed turbid water downstream (p. 10).
- Incidental damage: a mature western red cedar and a young western hemlock were felled by the excavator bucket; roadside mountain alder saplings repeatedly struck (p. 8).
- Nothing on fish presence upstream of the crossings.

## Recommendations and priorities
No recommendations on crossings or priorities. The report lists the mitigations applied (timing window, dewatering and fish monitoring, minimising riparian damage, pausing works when turbidity rose, fish screens on intakes, turbidity monitoring) (p. 11). No costs given.

## Useful for crossing work today
Both wood box culverts on Smoke Creek FSR branches 5 and 7 were removed in September 2025 as part of deactivation, so any legacy PSCIS or FSR crossing record at these UTMs should now be treated as an open channel, not a candidate for replacement. Neither location matched a crossing within 300 m in the local LNTH/UNTH PSCIS extracts, so the sites are probably not in PSCIS at all and add no barrier data. The only habitat signal is the low-flow, cobble/gravel, headwater character of the stream and the absence of any fish observation during dewatering.
