# 24174 — 2008/2009 Fish Presence/Absence Mapping Progress Report (Phases 1 through 3)
- **Author / organisation:** Jenine Mylymok and Michele Trumbley, RPBio, Trumbley Environmental Consulting Ltd.; digital-capture guidelines by Tannis Nelson, Forsite Consultants Ltd. Prepared for Kamloops Timber Supply Area 11 (TSA 11) licensees. The original 2006/2007 report was by Snowy River Resources Ltd. (Dec 6, 2007) and was updated by TEC with permission (p. 1).   **Year:** 2009 (draft Mar 31, final Apr 7, 2009)   **Funder / program (FIA, FRBC, DFO …):** Forest Investment Account (p. 1)
- **Region check:** in the North Thompson (LNTH) and beyond — covers the whole Kamloops TSA 11 including TFL 18 and TFL 35. Phase 3 (2008/09) mapped the Adams River watershed (BCTS, Gilbert Smith, Interfor, Stella Jones) and the Lower North Thompson watershed (BCTS, Canfor, Tolko, Weyerhaeuser); Phase 2 (2007/08) was TFL 18 (Canfor); Phase 1 (2006/07) was the Barriere, Deadman, Raft, Tranquille and Upper Adams River watersheds (Figure 1 p. 3). TFL 35 "lies entirely within the Lower North Thompson Watershed" (p. 1). Note: the L. North Thompson Phase 3 hardcopy mapsheets were quality-assured but not digitized in 2009 (p. 3).
- **Type:** other — fish presence/absence mapping, a planning tool for selecting FPCI sites; no crossings assessed

## What was done
- 65 mapsheets in 2008/09 (50 new priority sheets plus additions to 15 earlier sheets); 10 in 2007/08 (TFL 18); 48 in 2006/07; 105 mapsheets total (p. 1, Figure 1 p. 3). Mapsheets were chosen using the MOE "Fisheries Value" polygon ranking of watershed groups and sub-basins.
- Known fish presence, absence, distribution and sampling data were compiled from FISS, Habitat Wizard, consultant stream-classification (FSID) assessments, RISC FHAP and 1:20,000 FFHI reconnaissance inventories, MOE/DFO fish distribution reports, and communication with Steve Maricle (MOE) on small lakes (p. 2). Data were hand-mapped on 1:20,000 TRIM sheets and entered in an Excel "Kamloops TSA Fish Presence/Absence Database" keyed by point (P#) and line (L#) references; Forsite digitized the arcs and points into a geodatabase with PDF maps (pp. 4, 8–11). Source reports in digital form were delivered on a CD.
- Coding convention (Table 1 p. 5): only confirmed fish presence (solid red) and confirmed absence (solid blue) are drawn; all other watercourses under 20 % gradient are assumed fish streams and left uncoded. Reach breaks are placed only where confirmed presence changes to confirmed absence on the same stream (Table 2 p. 6).

## Findings
No barrier or crossing results — the report is methodological. Two interpretive rules are stated (p. 7): the outlet of a fish-bearing lake is coded fish-bearing only if sampling in the outlet caught fish; and wetlands, streams and lakes upstream of a confirmed-absence (blue) reach should be assumed non-fish-bearing. All source data were verified by Michele Trumbley as FPC/FRPA compliant (p. 4).

## Recommendations and priorities
None on crossings. The maps and database are intended to be overlaid on licensee road networks to pick FPCI candidate sites (p. 2).

## Useful for crossing work today
No crossing data and nothing in PSCIS from this report. Its value is as the provenance of the fish-presence calls used in TEC's 2008 FPCI reports (e.g. 24097 cites the "fish-mapping portion of the Kamloops TSA 11 FPCI Project") and as a pointer to a compiled Lower North Thompson / Barriere / Raft fish presence-absence database (Excel plus geodatabase, current to Mar 31, 2009) that, if it can be obtained from Canfor, Tolko, BCTS or Forsite, would supplement FISS for confirming fish upstream of crossings. The Lower North Thompson sheets were never digitized in this phase, so that portion exists only as hardcopy.
