# 24379 — Assessment of Non-Natural Coho Barriers in the North Thompson Watershed (Forest Investment Account Project 4205014)
- **Author / organisation:** Shawn Clough, Secwepemc Fisheries Commission (Shuswap Nation Tribal Council), Kamloops; project supervisor Mike Galesloot, RPBio; field technicians Shawn Clough and Aaron Gillespie. Prepared for Tolko Industries Ltd., Louis Creek Division (Michael Bragg). Mapping by Cascadia Natural Resource Consultants. Initiated with the North Thompson Indian Band (Simpcw).   **Year:** January 2004 (field Aug–Oct 2003; titled "Third Quarter Report 2002-2003"; FIA fiscal year 2003/2004)   **Funder / program (FIA, FRBC, DFO …):** Forest Investment Account, Restoration and Rehabilitation Component, Aquatic Activity Area, project 4205014, through Tolko
- **Region check:** in the North Thompson — both UNTH and LNTH. Six units between Barriere and Valemount, all coho-producing (Appendix 5): Albreda River (WSC 129-838900), Blue River (129-696400), Fennell Creek (129-190100-69200, headwaters of the Barriere River), Lemieux Creek (129-281900, near Little Fort), North Thompson River upstream of the Albreda confluence (129, to the Thompson Glacier), and Reg Christie Creek (129-461200, between the Clearwater River and Little Hell's Gate) (pp. 2–3). Lion Creek (below Little Hell's Gate) was planned but omitted due to freeze-up (p. 6).
- **Type:** fish passage assessment (adapted FPCI, Fish Passage – Culvert Inspection Procedures, WRP Technical Circular 11, Parker 2000), coho-only; no fish sampling

## What was done
- Rationale: these five tributaries (excluding mainstem production) accounted for 38 % of North Thompson coho escapement in 2001 (DFO escapement database); Interior Fraser coho were recommended as endangered under SARA (pp. 1–3).
- Four-step process (p. 4): compile FISS/FRBC inventory/FHAP/MWLAP/DFO data and licensee/MOF maps; identify all crossings on coho streams; field-verify each is a culvert; confirm the channel is viable coho habitat. Crown forest roads only; private and MOTH crossings not assessed. Adaptation (no fish sampling, coho-only species use) was approved by the FIA Investment Manager.
- Office review found 69 crossings needing a field visit; 8 received full FPCI (Form A, Appendix 2), 59 needed no assessment (Form D, Appendix 3), 2 could not be assessed (Appendix 5 p. 2). Field work was delayed by the 2003 fire season and ran Aug 27 – Oct 31, 2003.
- Per watershed (pp. 6–12): Albreda 12 crossings identified, 2 FPCI, 9 bridges, 1 inaccessible (Site 7, Dora Creek, overgrown road). Blue River 4 identified, 0 FPCI, 2 bridges, 2 too steep. Fennell Creek 12 identified, 3 FPCI, 3 bridges, 3 dry, 2 >30 % gradient, 1 culvert removed. Lemieux 3 identified, 0 FPCI (1 bridge, 1 deactivated, 1 no culvert); most Lemieux crossings are bridges on private/MOTH land. Upper North Thompson 38 identified, 3 FPCI, 12 bridges, 7 no defined channel, 5 dry, 1 gradient/debris, 9 not coho habitat, 1 blocked (Site 7A, Pleasant Creek). Reg Christie: no Crown crossings within the coho reach (access limited by falls 600 m from the North Thompson); one possible private-land crossing not assessed.
- Roads named: Allan Creek FSR, Albreda River FSR, North Barriere FSR, a non-status road off North Barriere FSR, North Thompson FSR; also bridges on Clemina, Dora, Dominion, Allan, White, Blue River North, Barriere River, Lampriere, Canvas and Manteau creeks (Form D). Locations are given as lat/long DMS and 1:20,000 mapsheet, not UTM.

## Findings
FPCI Summary Table (Table 1 p. 5): 5 high, 2 moderate, 1 low priority culverts.
- Albreda Site 1, unnamed trib, Allan Creek FSR (52°31'33.6"N 119°07'15.6"W; 83D.045): 400 mm CSP, 33 cm outlet drop, 7 % slope, 1.35 m/s, stump partly blocking outlet, excavated inlet sump creating a 40 cm drop — full barrier, score 39 High, ~350 m (70 %) of habitat above; road responsibility BCTS Clearwater; excellent rearing habitat but no sampling (Form A pp. 1–4).
- Albreda Site 2, Albreda River FSR (83D.065): 1200 mm CSP, backflooded, cobble-filled — undetermined, score 25 Low, ~450 m above; recommend leave as is (pp. 5–8).
- Fennell Site 4, unnamed trib, North Barriere FSR (51°24'57.6"N 119°39'32.4"W; 82M.005): 800 mm CSP, no drop, 3 % slope, outlet ~60 % blocked by grading spoil — partial barrier (velocity), score 42 High, 1,250 m (77 %); but a 32.5 % gradient 40 m downstream is the controlling barrier; propose leaving as is (pp. 9–12).
- Fennell Site 5, Fennell Creek mainstem, non-status road off North Barriere FSR (51°26'42.0"N 119°38'45.6"W): collapsed log box culvert, ponding to 190 cm high-water mark, undetermined barrier, score 41 High, 2,625 m above (12 %), "requires removal ASAP" as an imminent sediment source; excellent rearing/spawning habitat immediately upstream; Fennell Creek contains RB, BT, CO, SK, KO (pp. 13–16).
- Fennell Site 6, Fennell Creek mainstem, same road (51°27'57.6"N 119°38'24.0"W): second collapsed log box culvert, creek dry at survey (beaver dam at Fennell Lake outlet), score 29 Moderate, 250 m (1 %), removal required (pp. 17–20).
- Upper North Thompson Site 12, unnamed trib, North Thompson FSR (52°30'32.4"N 119°22'15.6"W; 83D.054): 1000 mm CSP, 35 cm drop, 9 % slope, 2.1 m/s — full barrier, score 29 Moderate, 500 m (18 %), Weyerhaeuser Vavenby road; logging debris jams 5 m above culvert; coho suspected (FFHIP 1997/98) not confirmed (pp. 21–24).
- UNT Site 16 (52°28'58.8"N 119°17'16.8"W): 900 mm CSP, 60 cm drop, 6 % — full barrier, score 39 High, 300 m (75 %); downstream debris jams may already limit coho (pp. 25–28).
- UNT Site 20 (52°29'02.4"N 119°15'03.6"W): 900 mm CSP, no drop, 4 %, 0.8 m/s — partial (juvenile velocity) barrier, score 42 High, ~1,000 m (83 %); downstream pond connects to the North Thompson mainstem (pp. 29–32).
- Not assessed but high priority for 2004: Albreda Site 7 (Dora Creek, suspected coho, ATV access only) and UNT Site 7A, Pleasant Creek elliptical culvert blocked by a debris flow diverting the creek down the FSR; FFHIP shows coho in the reach with 150 m more habitat above; Clearwater Forest District notified Oct 30, 2003 (Form D pp. 1, 4).
- Authors' caveat (p. 6): high scores rest on assumed multiple species; only Fennell 5 and 6 need immediate action; the other culverts "may not actually present a barrier" and need fish presence/distribution sampling first. Every culvert assessed failed the FPCI Q100 sizing estimate. A log jam near the 12 km mark of Fennell Creek may block >10 km of high-quality habitat (p. 9). Blue River, Lemieux Creek and Reg Christie Creek have no Crown crossings needing work.

## Recommendations and priorities
In order (p. 13; Appendix 5): 1) deactivation/rehabilitation prescription for Fennell Creek Sites 5 and 6 ($3,500); 2) removal of both collapsed box culverts and channel rehabilitation ($10,500); 3) fish passage assessment of the possible log-jam barrier at 12 km on Fennell Creek ($1,000); 4) FPCI of Albreda Site 7 and UNT Site 7A ($1,800); 5) fish presence/distribution sampling on creeks with identified barriers ($5,250); 6) FPCI of the Lion Creek watershed ($2,500). Total 2003 project cost $12,906 (Appendix 5 p. 2).

## Useful for crossing work today
This is the earliest coho-specific barrier screen for the North Thompson and it predates PSCIS and the 2008 protocol, so its eight Form A sites are probably not in PSCIS under these ids; they are located by lat/long DMS and road km and would need to be matched to PSCIS by position (North Thompson FSR sites 12, 16, 20; Allan Creek FSR site 1; North Barriere FSR site 4). Two things to check first: whether the two collapsed log box culverts on the non-status road across Fennell Creek were removed after 2004, and whether the Pleasant Creek culvert (Site 7A, known coho) was ever assessed. The report's watershed-level conclusions — Blue River, Lemieux and Reg Christie have no Crown forest-road culvert problems in coho reaches, and most tributary crossings on the upper North Thompson are bridges or on non-habitat — are useful for de-prioritising model-flagged crossings there, while the Lion Creek and Fennell Creek 12 km log-jam questions remain open in this report.
