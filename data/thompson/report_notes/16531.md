# 16531 — 2005 Fish Sampling & Habitat Compensation Assessment For Clearwater Mile 59.0
- **Author / organisation:** Sean Sharpe, M.Sc., R.P.Bio, Silvatech Consulting Ltd. (Salmon Arm); hard-structure assessment by Alan Bates, P.Eng.; for Canadian National Railway Company   **Year:** November 2005 (field: July 4–7 and 11; October 13 and October 30–November 1, 2005)   **Funder / program (FIA, FRBC, DFO …):** CN Rail (proponent-funded compensation monitoring under a DFO authorization); not FIA/FRBC
- **Region check:** in the North Thompson. North Thompson River mainstem side channels at CN Mile 59.0, Clearwater Subdivision, private land (Cross property). No place name or coordinates in the text; the 2008 report cites the WSC Birch Island gauge, placing the reach in the Clearwater–Birch Island area. LNTH vs UNTH not stated. No tributary streams covered.
- **Type:** other — habitat-compensation effectiveness monitoring; year 2 of the 5-year CN Mile 59 series (16391 = 2004, 16850 = 2007, 17242 = 2008 final)

## What was done
Same design as 2004 (pp. 2–4): water quality at 25 minnow stations in July and October; hard-structure inspection by Alan Bates on July 11 and October 13, 2005 at high and low water; soft-structure habitat rating; 3 baited minnow traps per station for ~24 h, July 4–7 (70 trap-days) and October 30–November 1 (72 trap-days); ~5 h angling (lures and fly) for bull trout, rainbow and adult salmon; redd search in October; revegetation survey. Also a check of the west (right) bank upstream of the works, as recommended in 2004. No road crossings or streams other than the North Thompson and its Cross backchannel are named.

## Findings
- Fish (pp. 9–11, Table 3): July — 35 juvenile coho (0.5/trap-day), total catch only 52 fish (0.74/trap-day) because redside shiners were absent, versus 337 in August 2004; sculpin and a ~20 cm rainbow trout also taken. October — 95 coho and 12 Chinook juveniles (1.49/trap-day combined), total 149 fish. Roughly two-thirds of coho came from small pools beside root wads and stumps with vegetation cover; the small north channel (stations 23, 9–11) and log-jam pools (stations 2–3, 5–6) again most productive; none in the deep armoured section (structures 1–10).
- Angling (p. 12): several bull trout in the North Thompson back-eddy at the intake groyne; an adult rainbow trout and a Dolly Varden/bull trout in the deep armoured channel; a >25 cm bull trout near structure 1; rainbow fry in traps suggest trout spawning in the channel.
- Spawning (p. 13): **2 adult coho on a redd near Structure 15** in late October — first confirmed coho spawning in the new backchannel. In the Cross backchannel, ice-jam flood sediment covered almost all spawning gravels; no fish seen there in October.
- Structures (pp. 5–7): rock sieve further settled and plugged with small debris — 0.6 m head drop across it in October (0.4 m in 2004); 600 mm pipe still not visible, possibly crushed or buried, with a riprap block possibly on its outlet; sieve still judged passable for adult coho at spawning flows; inlet culvert not a barrier at moderate–low water (28 cm/s low-water velocity); sedimentation filling pools below the culvert; a large woody debris jam and bank erosion at the Cross backchannel confluence noted as a candidate compensation site.
- Water quality suitable; summer temperatures cooler than 2004. Planted vegetation >80 % survival; natural regeneration ~90 % cover on silt deposits; cattle access reduced but not eliminated.

## Recommendations and priorities
(pp. 15–16) Clear debris from and assess the rock sieve at the coming low-water season, including adult coho passage at low–moderate flows and keeping the inlet culvert as the design flow control; consider improving the log deflector boom; continue monitoring; future structures should be large log-jam or root-wad complexes beside 1–3 m² pools 50 cm–1 m deep; survey channel depth/bathymetry next year because flood sediment is filling the deep overwintering channel and floods removed some structures (e.g. the log jam in the armoured north channel). No costs given.

## Useful for crossing work today
Not a road-crossing report and not PSCIS material; the culverts discussed are the engineered intake to a compensation channel. For North Thompson planning it is evidence of coho spawning and rearing, Chinook rearing, and bull trout and rainbow presence in mainstem side channels at CN Mile 59 in 2005, which supports treating tributaries entering this reach as coho-accessible. Use it as fish-presence context, not as a source of barriers or priorities.
