## What was done
Fifth and final monitoring year required by the authorization (p. 1). 29 minnow-trap stations (23 in Site C, 1 on the mainstem, 1 at Site A, 4 at Site B), 3 baited traps per station for ~24 h, July 10–13 (72 trap-days in Site C) and October 29–November 2, 2008; water quality at each station; hard-structure inspection with video/photo; soft-structure rating; ~5 h angling per session; redd search in October; revegetation survey (pp. 4–5). Presents the five-year fish-capture table for Site C (p. 14). No road crossings named.

## Findings
- Five-year Site C captures (p. 14; ~equal effort, 23 stations, 60–69 trap-days per session):

  | Year | Jul/Aug coho | Jul/Aug Chinook | Jul/Aug total | Oct/Nov coho | Oct/Nov Chinook | Oct/Nov total |
  |---|---|---|---|---|---|---|
  | 2004 | 28 | – | 337 | 74 | – | 131 |
  | 2005 | 35 | – | 52 | 95 | – | 149 |
  | 2006 | 54 | – | 71 | 114 | 1 | 210 |
  | 2007 | 30 | 2 | 42 | 99 | 16 | 193 |
  | 2008 | 91 | 8 | 104 | 175 | 25 | 216 |

  2008: July 91 coho, 8 Chinook, 3 sculpin, 1 bull trout, 1 redside shiner in 72 trap-days (1.3 coho/trap-day, best July in 5 years); October 175 coho and 25 Chinook, 2.4 coho/trap-day, a five-year high (p. 15). Coho and Chinook catch per effort increased over the 5 years and captures spread from isolated pockets to the whole site; root-wad/pool complexes still ~2/3 of coho.
- Cross back channel (p. 16): 5 coho in July; 22 coho and 3 Chinook in October 2008 (2007: 1 and 11), plus shiners and sculpin, mostly beside root wads at Site B.
- Angling (p. 24): several bull trout and rainbow trout in the mainstem back-eddy at the intake groyne; nothing by angling in July. No spawning fish or redds in Site C in October 2008 (2 coho pairs had dug redds in 2007). Cross back channel gravels partly re-exposed by the 2008 freshet.
- Structures (pp. 1–2, 25): 600 mm intake pipe still absent/buried; passage at the intake limited at low water in 2008; low-water inlet velocity 14 cm/s; overwinter channel infilling. Site A riprap slid and filter fabric exposed; Site B stable and functioning. Water quality suitable. Willow survival 60–~100 % (avg ~80 %) with strong natural alder and sedge; Site B dyke plantings largely failed.

## Recommendations and priorities
(pp. 25, 28) Restoring the culvert connecting the intake structure to the North Thompson is "a high priority to meet the conditions of the authorization" and should be done as soon as feasible in 2009 (tentatively spring 2009 pending operating plans and landowner agreement), then monitored for 2 years; one more season of fish assessment after the intake is restored; rebury exposed filter fabric at Site A; monitor bank erosion between Sites A and B annually; replant and monitor the Site B dyke for 2 years; no further riparian or habitat-structure monitoring needed at Site C; bathymetric survey of the overwinter channel; if LWD is available, place unanchored root wads in the channel; future structures to be large log-jam/root-wad complexes beside 1–3 m² pools. No costs given.

## Useful for crossing work today
Not a crossing inventory and not PSCIS data; the culvert in question is an engineered side-channel intake on the North Thompson mainstem. As the series summary it gives a planner the strongest single statement of Interior Fraser coho and Chinook rearing use of North Thompson side channels at CN Mile 59 (2004–2008 five-year trend), with coho spawning observed in 2005 and 2007 and bull trout and rainbow trout present — context for treating tributary crossings entering this reach as within coho/Chinook habitat. It also flags that the intake fish-passage pipe was buried by 2004 and never restored during the monitoring period, an example of an engineered passage feature failing within a year of construction.
