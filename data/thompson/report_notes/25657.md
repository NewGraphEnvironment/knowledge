(DFO Restoration Engineer, Southern Interior); recipient Tolko Industries Ltd., Heffley Cr. Division   **Year:** fiscal 2004/2005 (construction August 10–14, 2004)   **Funder / program (FIA, FRBC, DFO …):** FIA — Investment Schedule # 4350-004, Project Number 4350-004; design input from DFO
- **Region check:** in the North Thompson (LNTH). Watershed – Barriere River; sub-basin – Fennell Creek (watershed code 129-190100-69200), "in the North Thompson River valley approximately 105 km northeast of Kamloops". Sites are on the North Barriere Lake Forest Service Road.
- **Type:** remediation (culvert removal and habitat restoration), completion abstract

## What was done
A two-page FIA project completion abstract. Two failing wood culverts on Fennell Creek, identified as fish-passage barriers in the *Assessment of Non-Natural Coho Barriers in the North Thompson Watershed* (Clough, 2004; FIA Project 4205014), were removed and the sites restored:

- **Site 5 @ 40.9 km North Barriere Lake FSR**
- **Site 6 @ 43.9 km North Barriere Lake FSR**

Objectives were (1) develop a culvert removal and fish habitat restoration design and (2) remove the culverts and restore unobstructed fish passage while enhancing juvenile salmonid habitat. The design used adapted FIA Aquatic Restoration and Rehabilitation Standards; because the sites were isolated (roads inactive, riparian community re-established) the design used on-site materials. After the culverts were removed, post-and-brush revetments were built from locally collected cottonwood and conifer, with cedar stringers salvaged from the wood culverts (24–30 in. diameter) as deadmen and some local rock for ballast. Work was completed within the approved fish construction window, August 10–14, 2004.

Streams named: Fennell Creek (Barriere River watershed). Roads named: North Barriere Lake FSR. No PSCIS crossing ids are given.

## Findings
- The wood culverts were 1960s-era, blocking fish passage and holding "significant volumes of fine sediments" that would have entered the stream on catastrophic failure; this threat was removed.
- Fennell Creek is described as "an important coho production stream to the North Thompson coho unit" and locally important for resident rainbow and bull trout. Interior Fraser coho are noted as proposed for endangered listing under SARA.
- Outputs: 31 m of bank protected and 80 m² of habitat created by the two post-and-brush revetments.
- No fish sampling results are reported in the abstract.

## Recommendations and priorities
- Cost summary: Labour $8,340; Travel $979; Materials $599; Equipment $3,349; **Total $13,267**.
- Recommended post-completion "routine monitoring" (Gadboury & Wong 1999; Koning et al. 1998): inspect all structures after one spring freshet and then every five years or after a 1-in-10-year flood; Gee-trap and/or electrofishing surveys to document juvenile and adult use of the structures. Objectives: confirm the works function as intended, identify remedial needs, evaluate the need for detailed monitoring.

## Useful for crossing work today
This documents that the two Fennell Creek culverts at 40.9 km and 43.9 km on the North Barriere Lake FSR were removed in August 2004, so any PSCIS or historic barrier record for those sites should be treated as remediated (road deactivated, culverts gone, revetments in place). It also points to the source inventory — Clough (2004) *Assessment of Non-Natural Coho Barriers in the North Thompson Watershed* (FIA 4205014) — as the list from which these were prioritised; other barriers from that assessment may remain unaddressed. Whether the removals were ever entered into PSCIS as remediations is not stated; worth checking. The recommended follow-up monitoring (post-freshet inspection, fish sampling) is not reported here, so the effectiveness of the works is undocumented in this abstract.
