- **Author / organisation:** Sean Sharpe, M.Sc., R.P.Bio and Alan Bates, P.Eng., Silvatech Consulting Ltd. (Salmon Arm), for Canadian National Railway Company   **Year:** December 2004 (field: August 9–12, September 28, October 28–30, 2004)   **Funder / program (FIA, FRBC, DFO …):** CN Rail (proponent-funded compensation monitoring under a DFO authorization); not FIA/FRBC
- **Region check:** in the North Thompson. North Thompson River mainstem and its side channels at CN Mile 59.0, Clearwater Subdivision, on private land (Cross property; landowner Don Cross). The report gives no place name beyond "Mile 59" and no coordinates; the 2008 report in the series references the WSC Birch Island gauge, so the reach is in the Clearwater–Birch Island area. Which watershed group (LNTH vs UNTH) it falls in is not stated in the text. No tributary streams are covered.
- **Type:** other — habitat-compensation effectiveness monitoring (fish sampling, structure inspection, revegetation) for CN riprap/dyke works; year 1 of a 5-year series (see also 16531 = 2005, 16850 = 2007, 17242 = 2008 final)

## What was done
Series context: in 2003 CN built a riprap dyke, groynes and berms on the North Thompson River; compensation was an inlet culvert feeding a constructed "new backchannel" (later called Site C) downstream of the dyke with habitat structures and riparian planting, plus bank stabilization on the "Cross backchannel" immediately upstream (Site A; root wads added August 2004). Silvatech was retained to assess whether the works created coho rearing/spawning habitat (p. 2).

2004 work (pp. 3–4): water quality (temperature, DO, pH, conductivity) at 25 minnow-trap stations; hard-structure inspection by Alan Bates on September 28, 2004 (dyke, groynes, finger dyke, log boom, rock sieve, inlet culvert) with flow-velocity measurements; soft-structure (log/root-wad/rock) habitat rating; fish sampling with 3 baited minnow traps per station, ~24 h sets, August 11–13 and October 29–30, 2004 (65 and 64 trap-days in the compensation area); angling (~4 h) for bull trout/rainbow/adult salmon; redd search in October; revegetation survey. No crossings or roads are named; the only "culvert" is the compensation channel's inlet culvert, not a road crossing.

## Findings
- Fish (pp. 11–14, Table 3): August — 29 juvenile coho, 0.45 coho/trap-day; total catch 337 (5.2/trap-day), dominated by redside shiners (many with black-spot parasite); also suckers. October — 74 coho (1.2/trap-day), Chinook juveniles appear, total 131 fish. Coho concentrated in the small north channel with mature riparian cover (stations 23, 9–11) and around log-jam pools (stations 2–3, 5–6); none in the deep, low-cover northwest armoured section (structures 1–10).
- Angling: one bull trout in the North Thompson back-eddy at the intake groyne; a rainbow trout seen below the riffle near station 10; ~1 cm trout fry near structures 22–23; 2 unidentified adult salmon (jack Chinook or coho) in the pool near Structure 21 in October.
- Spawning: no coho redds in the new backchannel by late October (run likely not yet arrived); 7 redds (likely Chinook) in the Cross backchannel near station 25 (p. 14). Suitable coho spawning gravels between stations 9–11, 14–15 and below structures 21–22.
- Structures (pp. 6–9): dyke/groynes stable; log boom high and dry at low flow; rock sieve settled and collecting small debris; the 600 mm fish-passage pipe at the base of the sieve not visible, possibly buried; inlet culvert velocities (45 cm/s at low water) acceptable for adult coho; fish access "available but difficult" at the inlet, excellent at the downstream confluence. Seepage through the main dyke feeding a silting side channel.
- Water quality within coho optima both seasons; risk is isolation of fry in shallow pools at falling water.
- Revegetation: willow stakes surviving well near the channel; cattle, beaver and deer damage.

## Recommendations and priorities
(p. 17) Continue monitoring; winter site visit for temperature/DO under ice; **priority: inspect the 600 mm submerged culvert at the intake at low water**; check both backchannels for spawning in late November–December; future mark–recapture to establish origin and fate of fry; future habitat work should be large log-jam/root-wad complexes next to pools, not single logs; further enhancement most logical downstream of the culvert outlet. No costs given.

## Useful for crossing work today
This is not a road-crossing assessment; the only culvert is the engineered inlet to a compensation side channel on the North Thompson mainstem, and it will not be in PSCIS. Its value for passage planning is contextual: it confirms juvenile coho, Chinook, bull trout and rainbow trout rearing in mainstem side channels at Mile 59, and that the Cross backchannel is a Chinook (and reportedly coho) spawning channel — i.e. any tributary crossings in this reach are within a coho/Chinook production area. The series is Interior Fraser coho evidence, not a source of barrier records.
