# 24146 — FIA Project #9028007 Louis Creek Fish Passage Assessments
- **Author / organisation:** Triton Environmental Consultants Ltd., Kamloops (project 4243, revision 1). Prepared for Tolko Industries Ltd., Heffley Creek Division (attn. Michael Bragg, Regional Forester).   **Year:** 2010 (Jan 31, 2010; field work Nov 2009)   **Funder / program (FIA, FRBC, DFO …):** Forest Investment Account, project 9028007
- **Region check:** in the North Thompson (LNTH). Louis Creek watershed, a tributary to the North Thompson River about 58 km north of Kamloops on Highway 5, accessed via Agate Bay Road (p. 2). Streams named: Louis Creek, unnamed tributaries to Louis Creek, Fraser Creek (tributary to Louis Creek) and its tributaries; Graham Creek is the southern starting point of the route (p. 4).
- **Type:** fish passage assessment (closed-bottom structures under the MOE 2009 3rd-edition "Strategic Approach" planning protocol and "Field Assessment for Fish Passage Determination of Closed Bottomed Structures")

## What was done
- Tolko supplied a 1:80,000 working map with 101 potential crossings from the MoE GIS model on FSR, permitted and non-status roads (p. 3). Triton screened them against ARC Environmental's 1999/2000 1:20,000 Fish and Fish Habitat Inventory of Heffley and Louis Creek watersheds: 12 no visible channel (NVC), 66 suspected non-fish-bearing, 1 confirmed non-fish-bearing, 12 suspected fish-bearing (1 duplicate, 1 private road dropped), 10 confirmed fish-bearing (4 duplicates dropped) — 16 sites eligible (Table 1 p. 3; rationale for every site in Appendix 1). Fourteen mapped crossings had no road at all; FSR 9086 01 appeared on two road layers and produced duplicates (p. 4).
- A three-person crew (2 biologists, 1 RPBio) visited 15 of the 16 sites in November 2009 with Garmin 60CSx GPS, clinometer, rod and level, and recorded the MoE culvert field data submission form (Appendix 2, .xls) (pp. 4–5).

## Findings
- Only 6 of the 16 sites had a structure: clear-span bridges over Louis Creek at sites 13 (10/707533/5654084) and 44 (10/706903/5562863 as printed), round culverts at sites 53 and 65, and two culverts carrying irrigation pipe through hayfields at sites 60 and 62 on "Fraser Creek" (Table 2 p. 5). Ten sites had no structure; 8 of those were NVC (p. 6).
- One barrier: site 53, tributary to Louis Creek at 10/706138/5663497, round culvert with a 145 cm outlet drop and 4.7 % slope, Fish Barrier Scoring Table score >30 (pp. 6–7). Channel dry 200 m up and 400 m downstream at the time, apparently ephemeral; habitat value low; interpretive maps suggest ~600–632 m of habitat upstream and potential salmon/sport fish use. Post-assessment it was found to be on a private road within woodlot 316, so FIA-ineligible; Tolko has no harvest plans there and will not maintain the road (pp. 6, 8). Sites 26–39 upstream of it were excluded as "upstream of barrier (site 53)" (Appendix 1).
- No partial barriers. Sites 15–21 were excluded as upstream of a natural falls barrier (Appendix 1). Bridges at 13 and 44 appeared in good condition (non-engineering observation). Site 65 is a ditch culvert under a private driveway. No fish sampling was done and no species were observed; the watershed is described as supporting salmon, sport and regionally significant species (p. 2).

## Recommendations and priorities
No restoration or implementation plan was required: "No sites were assessed that both presented a barrier or potential barrier to fish passage and was FIA eligible" (p. i). Should site 53 ever be considered, spring fish-presence sampling is needed first and the 4 %/8 % channel gradients make it a difficult site (p. 8). Process recommendations (p. 9): verify road names and tenure before field work, remove duplicate and phantom crossings from the GIS model, and incorporate fish inventory data and future harvest plans in prioritisation. Costs: none given.

## Useful for crossing work today
For Louis Creek the take-away is a negative result: of 101 modelled crossings only 16 were on possibly fish-bearing streams and only one culvert (private woodlot road, ephemeral tributary, 145 cm drop) was a barrier; Crown-road crossings of Louis Creek itself are bridges. The field data went to MoE on the 2009 culvert submission form, so sites 53, 60, 62 and 65 (and the bridges) are probably in PSCIS with Nov 2009 dates. The report says nothing about the many private-land and highway crossings in the valley bottom, which is where remaining passage questions on Louis Creek would lie; Appendix 1 gives coordinates and a fish-bearing class for all 101 modelled sites, which is useful for confirming that a modelled crossing is a duplicate or on a >20 % reach.
