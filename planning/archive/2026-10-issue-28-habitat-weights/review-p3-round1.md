# Review p3 round 1: five new BT rows (data rows 482-486, file lines 483-487)

Rows numbered as data rows (482 = file line 483). Each row was checked against the PDF text, and against page renders where layout mattered.

## Findings

- **[severity: bug]** row 486 (kind none): "None gives one" is wrong for gradient. Isaak et al. 2015 Appendix S1 (PDF page 15 of 27, in the same Zotero PDF and in the extracted text near the "Cumulative distributions of trout occurrence" caption) has Fig. 1. It plots the cumulative proportion of sites against **reach slope (%)** from 0 to 15 for **juvenile bull trout (<150 mm, 1102 sites)**, with cutthroat trout (<125 mm, 943 sites) and brook trout (3061 sites) alongside. It is drawn from the regional electrofishing database (50-200 m reaches). That makes it a graded, reach-scale use distribution of juvenile BT by gradient. It is use-only, built from presence sites with no absences or availability, and it is a figure with no tabulated values. But it is exactly what the row says no source holds, and the sentence "Isaak 2015 fits slope at whole-habitat scale" leaves it out. Rendered and confirmed: about 0.6 of BT sites lie below 6 %, about 0.85 below 9 %, and about 0.95 below 12 %. The row needs restating, for example as "no graded occupancy (use vs non-use) curve at reach or segment scale; Isaak 2015 App. S1 Fig. 1 gives a use-only cumulative distribution by reach slope". An evidence row for the figure may also be worth adding. Row 96 cites only the 15 % trim sentence on p. 2542, not this figure.

- **[severity: bug]** row 486: "elevation appears only as a temperature proxy" is contradicted by Porter et al. 2008, which the row says it searched. Table 3.5 (printed p. 37) is the selected best-fit watershed-scale logistic model for BT occurrence in the Thompson EDU. It includes **Mean Elevation, coefficient +0.002 (SE 0.001), P = 0.005**: a fitted, graded, positive effect of elevation on BT occurrence, at watershed scale and not framed as a temperature proxy. At macroreach scale, the candidate models carried upstream elevation, downstream elevation and gradient (Table 3.13, printed p. 50). The selected BT model kept only sinuosity and bankfull width (Table 3.15, printed p. 51; gradient models ΔAIC > 100). That is a segment-scale null for both gradient and elevation, which the row could cite rather than only "Porter 2008 gives CART classes". The elevation clause should be scoped, for example to "no reach/segment-scale elevation curve; Porter 2008 fits mean watershed elevation (+) at watershed scale and drops reach elevation at macroreach scale". The same report's Fig. 3.16 (printed p. 69) plots juvenile BT CPUE against field-measured mean reach gradient at FDIS sites. It is a scatter rather than a curve, but it is also a reach-scale graded view that the "CART classes" summary omits.

- **[severity: fragile]** row 484 (Dunham & Chandler, p. 30): the note puts "explained 94 %" on p. 15. The sentence starts on printed p. 15, but "explaining 94% of the variance ... (F9,60 = 97.21 ...)" is printed on p. 16. Cite pp. 15-16. Also worth stating: the authors fit that model only for streams with both fish and temperature data in 2000, and call it preliminary.

- **[severity: fragile]** row 485 (Porter 2008, p. 66): "Same FDIS electrofishing data that scripts/0290-0291 use" overstates it. Porter used the FDIS juvenile electrofishing records available about 2007-08, for the Interior Freshwater Ecoregion. The 0290/0291 scripts use the province-wide FDIS export supplied in 2021-22. The datasets overlap and come from the same database, so the non-independence point stands, but they are not the same data. Suggest "drawn from the same FDIS database (Interior ecoregion subset, pre-2008), so not independent".
