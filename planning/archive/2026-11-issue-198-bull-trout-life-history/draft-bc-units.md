## How are the forms split across BC, and which units are listed?

Page numbers are printed pages, with three exceptions where the page is the PDF's: the two registry pages and the
2025 COSEWIC report, which the group library holds as prints of web pages with no printed numbers, and the 2012
report's cover.

**Three forms inland; a fourth, sea-run, only on the south coast.** Interior bull trout take the three forms. The
coastal lineage adds limited movement into salt water.

"In interior regions of western North America, the three general life history forms of bull trout are stream
resident, fluvial, and adfluvial. For bull trout of the coastal evolutionary lineage, limited movements into
saltwater also occur (anadromy), although few details of these movements are known" (hagen_decker2011StatusBull, p. 3)

The federal registry places the sea-run form on BC's south coast.

"The first three forms are common throughout the Canadian range, but the anadromous form is restricted to the south
coast of British Columbia." (speciesregistrycanada2020BullTrout, p. 6)

COSEWIC ties it to the southern genetic lineage.

"Genetic Lineage 1 contains the only anadromous (sea-going) Bull Trout" (post_etal2012COSEWICassessment, p. 17)

**The forms share watersheds.** A watershed can hold more than one form.

"Different life history forms often co-occur in the same watershed although some spatial segregation may occur."
(b_c_ministryofwaterlandandresourcestewardship2023ManagementPlan, p. 13)

Hagen and Decker say what usually keeps a resident population apart from a migratory one.

"Stream resident populations are typically separated from migratory populations by an obstacle or barrier to
migration" (hagen_decker2011StatusBull, p. 3)

**Body size by form, in BC.** BC's fluvial fish are mostly smaller than its adfluvial fish, with exceptions in the
northern and upper-basin rivers.

"Most fluvial populations in B.C. are comprised of smaller individuals (means of 380 to 480 mm"
(b_c_ministryofwaterlandandresourcestewardship2023ManagementPlan, p. 13)

"but populations in the Stikine, Liard, Peace, upper Fraser (Pillipow and Williamson 2004), and upper Kootenay rivers
can be as large as adfluvial populations" (b_c_ministryofwaterlandandresourcestewardship2023ManagementPlan, p. 13)

**Reservoirs have made adfluvial populations, and may have cost fluvial ones.** Hagen and Decker, citing Hagen 2008:

"Some bull trout populations in BC have adapted well to living in large reservoirs, and in some cases where kokanee
have been introduced as a prey fish base growth and survival may be greater than in pre-existing riverine
conditions" (hagen_decker2011StatusBull, p. 7)

"and perhaps the loss of populations and genetic diversity (e.g. loss of fluvial form) as well"
(hagen_decker2011StatusBull, p. 7)

**Where bull trout are absent.** The BC plan, citing McPhail 2007 and Hagen and Decker 2011:

"Bull Trout are notably absent from most small coastal watersheds and from three adjacent, warm water drainages in
the southern interior: the Kettle, Okanagan, and Similkameen"
(b_c_ministryofwaterlandandresourcestewardship2023ManagementPlan, p. 14)

**Five designatable units, two of them on Schedule 1.** COSEWIC split Canada's bull trout into five units by
genetic lineage and biogeographic zone.

"this report recognizes five DUs for Bull Trout in Canada" (post_etal2012COSEWICassessment, p. 18)

Three of the five lie wholly or partly in BC and are named here.
- The South Coast unit (DU1, Genetic Lineage 1) holds the southern lineage. Its range:

  "range includes the Skagit River, Squamish River, Ryan River, Ure Creek, Lillooet River, Pitt Lake/River, Lower
  Fraser River (below Hell's Gate Canyon)"
  (post_etal2012COSEWICassessment, p. 23)

- The Western Arctic unit (DU2, Genetic Lineage 2) is the Mackenzie drainage, which in BC is the Peace and the
  Liard. Its range:

  "range includes Mackenzie River drainages including the Liard, Peace and Athabasca River basins." (post_etal2012COSEWICassessment, p. 23)

- The Pacific unit (DU5, Genetic Lineage 2) is the rest of BC's Pacific drainages. Its range:

  "range includes the Upper Columbia, Fraser above Hell's Gate Canyon, Homathko, Klinaklini, Skeena, Nass,
  Iskut-Stikine and Taku rivers." (post_etal2012COSEWICassessment, p. 24)

COSEWIC assessed the South Coast and Western Arctic units as Special Concern in 2012, and the Pacific unit as Not at
Risk. Both covers have no printed number, so their pages are the PDF's.

"South Coast British Columbia populations - SPECIAL CONCERN" (post_etal2012COSEWICassessment, p. 1)

"Pacific populations - NOT AT RISK" (post_etal2012COSEWICassessment, p. 1)

The 2025 reassessment kept the two units at Special Concern.

"South Coast British Columbia population - Special Concern" (cosewic2025COSEWICassessment, p. 1)

"Western Arctic population - Special Concern" (cosewic2025COSEWICassessment, p. 1)

It described the South Coast unit as five river systems that hold the sea-run form.

"This large freshwater fish is found in five river systems in southwestern British Columbia. It exhibits considerable
diversity in life history traits, including a unique anadromous form that migrates to the sea."
(cosewic2025COSEWICassessment, p. 5)

It described the Western Arctic unit across four jurisdictions.

"This large freshwater fish is distributed throughout northeastern British Columbia, southern Yukon, southwestern
Northwest Territories, and parts of Alberta." (cosewic2025COSEWICassessment, p. 6)

It moved the Pacific unit up from Not at Risk.

"Designated Not at Risk in November 2012. Status re-examined and designated Special Concern in May 2025."
(cosewic2025COSEWICassessment, p. 8)

Under the Species at Risk Act, the South Coast and Western Arctic units are on Schedule 1 as Special Concern. Both
registry pages give the same status:

"Status on Schedule 1 Special Concern" (speciesatriskpublicregistry2026BullTrout, p. 2)

"Status on Schedule 1 Special Concern" (speciesregistrycanada2020BullTrout, p. 2)

## Gaps

- No source read gives the share of BC populations by form, or a map of forms. Hagen and Decker 2011's Appendix 4
  names forms core area by core area, in comments; it is a table and is not quoted here.
- Neither registry page states whether the Pacific unit's 2025 status has led to a listing.
