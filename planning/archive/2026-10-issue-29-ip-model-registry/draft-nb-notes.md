### Vocabulary gaps (affect several models)

- **`lake_area` accepts only `ha`.** Woll 2015 (Lakes > 1.5 km2) and Woll 2017 (Lakes bigger than 2 km2) state
  km2, and the gate's value check has no km2 to ha rescale. Both rows are therefore `qual`, with the hectare
  figure in `note`. Adding `km2` to `lake_area` (×100) would let them carry values.
- **No `stream_order` flow metric.** Woll 2015 coho summer rearing and Woll 2017 Chinook and coho rearing size
  streams by Strahler order alone. Their registry rows are `flow_metric = none`, and `note` names the order.
- **Variables with no vocabulary entry**, recorded in notes and not as rows:
  - turbidity and glacial influence (Woll 2015, Woll 2017);
  - absolute floodplain width (Woll 2017);
  - tributary-confluence probability (Woll 2015);
  - distance from mouth (Woll 2017 pink);
  - obstacle height (Porter).

## woll2015 and woll2017: two models

They are two models, not one:
- different study areas: Mat-Su, and Nushagak-Kvichak;
- different years and reports;
- different variable sets;
- different life stages: 2015 is juvenile rearing only, split summer/winter, while 2017 covers spawning and
  rearing for five species;
- different stream-size variables: 2015 thresholds MAF, and 2017 thresholds "channel size" and stream order.

Same lead author, same NetMap/TNC lineage and the same Parks & Madison MAF regression, but no shared rule
table.

## porter2008: Thompson EDU rearing capacity

**Is it a threshold model?** Partly. The occurrence models are logistic regressions (not thresholds), adjusted
by barrier passage probability from obstacle height (Table 2.3: <2 m passes 1.0, >10 m 0.0). The rearing
capacity model is a rule set: CART splits of FDIS juvenile electrofishing CPUE on bankfull width and gradient,
turned into LOW, MEDIUM and HIGH classes per macroreach (p. 67). The authors call it "an index of the intrinsic
potential of that reach to provide high-quality rearing habitat" (p. 25).

I registered that capacity model only:
- no class is a 0-suitability edge, so every row is `hsi` with blank parameter;
- there is no access gradient, so no access rows.

Tags and new rows:
- **Tagged:** rows 101 and 108 (BT `hsi` rules).
- **Not tagged:**
  - 42, 43 and 44 are CH rules recorded as `dist`. Per the brief, I did not tag them; instead I wrote new CH
    `hsi` rows for the same rules (3.25 %, 15 m, 2.6 m);
  - 102 is a `qual` describing CPUE data, not a model rule.
- **New, other species:**
  - CO: 3.25 % LOW; 2.45-3.25 % HIGH; 1.75 m;
  - BT: >3.35 m HIGH;
  - RB: 0.85 %; 0.85-7.4 % and 2.05-8.05 m HIGH; ≥7.4 % MEDIUM. RB is juvenile O. mykiss in FDIS, with
    steelhead not separated.
- **Gradient direction:** steeper rates higher for CH and BT, and no rule is a maximum.

**Flow (pp. 21-22):**
- **Metric:** bankfull width, modelled for each 1:50K macroreach.
- **Equation:** Davies et al. (2007), Wb = 0.042 A^0.48 P^0.74, from drainage area (30 m DEM) and mean annual
  upstream precipitation (ClimateSource).
- **Calibration data:** Davies fitted the equation to **orthophoto** widths in Puget Sound. I listed the input
  as `field_measurement`, the nearest term in the vocabulary, and said so in `note`.
- **Check:** modelled width against 155 FDIS field widths, R² = 0.73 (p. 22).
- **CART fit:** the splits were fit on **field-measured** FDIS bankfull width and gradient, then applied to
  modelled widths. Width is described as "a cumulative indicator of mean annual stream flow", but no discharge
  is computed. No conversion.
- **Confinement:** tested, and not a predictor (p. 66). `valley_confinement` is `none`.

**Validation: `density`, negative.** 10-fold cross-validation of the trees on CPUE "failed to identify
statistically distinct separators of CPUE response for any of the four species using standard splitting
criteria" (p. 66). The classes come from relaxed complexity criteria, and the authors call them "not
statistically significant".
