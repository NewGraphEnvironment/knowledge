## 1. `agrawal2005`: registered

Agrawal et al. 2005 (NOAA-TM-NMFS-SWFSC-379). The text layer is a poor OCR and the body pages have no printed page
numbers, so pages are PDF pages (as in existing row 60). The coho, steelhead and Chinook suitability curves are
**printed only as Figure 1**, so its own rows are `qual`, one per species x variable (gradient, valley constraint,
discharge). The only numbers in its own text are:

- the coho temperature mask: lowest mean August **air** temperature (LMAT) 21, 21.5 and 22 degC, as three sensitivity
  layers (PDF p. 19). These are three `param` rows.
- Life-history context ("usually <2%-3% but occasionally up to 5%" for coho, "generally 2-7%" for steelhead). This is
  not a model rule, so I did not write a row for it.

The numbers come from **Sheer et al. 2009**, with `cited_source` "Agrawal et al. 2005":
- CO: max gradient 7% (p. 9), interior "very low IP" above 5%, min mean annual discharge 0.01 m3/s, max air temp
  21.5 degC.
- CH: 8% max (row 57, tagged), interior 3.5% (new), 0.28 m3/s (row 120, tagged), p. 13.
- ST: 12% max, p. 18.

**Access rows (a judgement call).** Sheer lists these maxima under "Specified natural barriers". Agrawal PDF p. 13
says "the upper extent of tiny positive values for gradient reflects the ability of adults to ascend reaches ... and
thus defines the range of accessible habitat". So I added `access` / `access_gradient_max` `param` rows (CO 7, CH 8,
ST 12) and one Agrawal `qual` access row per species. The same maxima also stay as rearing `hsi` curve ends.

**Stages.** Agrawal frames all three species as juvenile rearing. Plate 3 maps the Chinook IP as "spawning and
rearing", and row 60 is CH spawning, so CH has a spawning registry row. CO and ST spawning come from Bjorkstedt (below).

Steelhead are winter-run, which is in `note`. Agrawal does not model summer steelhead or spring Chinook.

**Not adopted:** a 24 degC mean August air temperature limit for steelhead (footnote 14, from Boughton,
unpublished). Agrawal "found no support for implementing a temperature-based constraint", so I wrote no row for it.

## 2. `bjorkstedt_etal2005analysishistorical`: applies Agrawal unchanged, so `model_id` `agrawal2005`

The evidence:
- p. 25: "we implemented a GIS model ... (Burnett et al., 2003, see Agrawal et al., 2005, for technical details regarding
  our implementation of the model)".
- p. 30: "An extensive literature search provided no basis for choosing alternative suitability curves for the
  NCCCRD (Agrawal et al., 2005)".
- p. 126, steelhead: available data "did not indicate strong support for choosing habitat suitability curves different
  from those developed for the Oregon Coast (Agrawal et al., 2005)".
- p. 89, Chinook: "The approach is identical to that used for coho salmon and steelhead (Burnett et al., 2003; Agrawal
  et al., 2005)".

It defines no thresholds of its own. Its rows (region "North-Central California Coast"):
- **CO and ST spawning, `qual` x 3 variables, and CH spawning valley constraint.** p. 26 says the IP is used "for
  spawning and rearing habitat of coho salmon and winter steelhead ... and for spawning habitat of fall-run Chinook".
  The variable list is on p. 25 ("we use mean gradient, mean annual discharge, and valley constraint"), more than 200
  tokens away, so the quote carries it as a `[bracketed insert]`. The p. 25 sentence is quoted in `note`.
  - Its steelhead chapter (p. 126) names rearing only, which `note` says. The CO and ST spawning registry rows rest on
    this document alone.
- **CH spawning discharge 50 m3/s (`hsi`, interior breakpoint, p. 89).** Of the two Chinook discharge curves it
  examined, the "conservative" one reduces spawning potential above 50 m3/s mean annual discharge (Plate 12), and the
  other has no upper limit.
  - Flag: I filed this under `agrawal2005` because Bjorkstedt describes its Chinook curves as Agrawal's. Rebellato's
    (tagged `rebellato2024`) "0.28-100" Chinook range attributed to Agrawal is consistent with a declining upper limb.
    But I could not confirm from text that 50 m3/s is Agrawal's own Figure 1 breakpoint. If the coordinator prefers,
    this one row could move to `bjorkstedt2005`, which would need its own CH-spawning registry row.
- **CO temperature mask 21.5 degC (p. 54), rearing and spawning.** "We therefore used 21.5 C as the threshold", which
  is Agrawal's middle layer, adopted.

## 4. `boughton_goslin2006`: registered

Over-summering juvenile steelhead (`rearing`). Table 5 (p. 9) is fitted separately for the South-Central and the
Southern California Coast ESU. I stored the **95% envelope**, which the authors recommend and use for all ten maps
(p. 10). The complete and consensus values are in each row's `note`.
- Per ESU: Q89 min/max (`rear_mad_min`/`rear_mad_max`), gradient min/max, VWI min/max, mean August air temp max, and
  mean annual air temp max. Those are `table` quotes.
- One `qual` row for the alluvial-substrate exclusion (p. 2).
- Mean annual temperature appears in Table 5, but the abstract and methods name only August temperature. `note` says
  so.

## Validation

- **agrawal2005: `none`.**
  - The Mad River natural-barrier check (PDF p. 20) is barrier detection, with no quantification ("we did not quantify
    the results").
  - The coho temperature mask was set from historical presence, which is calibration.
  - Bjorkstedt sets IP beside historical records but makes no reported comparison.
- **lindley2006: `none`.** I found no comparison of predicted reaches with fish observations in the paper.
- **boughton_goslin2006: `fish_observations`, qualitative only.**
  - The map notes (p. 18 onward) compare predictions with historical basin-level records and local knowledge. For
    example: "the model generally agreed with the historical record" on the southern Santa Barbara coast, with false
    positives and negatives elsewhere (Pacheco Creek, Big Sycamore Canyon).
  - There is no statistic. Fitting the envelopes to observations is calibration.
